Florence is the city that invented the Renaissance — and it still feels like it. Nowhere else in the world can you see Botticelli's Birth of Venus, Michelangelo's David, Brunelleschi's Dome, and the finest bistecca on earth all within 48 hours. The trick is knowing which museums to prioritise and which streets to escape into when the crowds overwhelm.

Top Attractions in Florence

1

Uffizi Gallery

Botticelli's Birth of Venus, da Vinci, Raphael, Caravaggio — all under one roof. Book at uffizi.it at least 2 weeks ahead. Allow 3–4 hours minimum and don't rush the Botticelli rooms.

2

The Duomo & Dome Climb

The cathedral is free. The dome climb (€30 combined pass, 463 steps) requires a timed booking at museumflorence.com. The panoramic view from the top is the finest in Florence — non-negotiable.

3

Michelangelo's David

Nothing prepares you for the scale. The David is not just large — it is enormous. Book at uffizi.it. Without a ticket, the queue can reach 2–3 hours. With one, you walk straight in.

4

Oltrarno & Boboli Gardens

Cross the Ponte Vecchio to the south bank. Florence's most liveable neighbourhood — artisan leather workshops, local wine bars, and the Boboli Gardens behind Pitti Palace (€10, refreshingly uncrowded).

The Arno & Ponte Vecchio

Florence's most iconic bridge has been lined with goldsmiths and jewellers since the 16th century. Cross it in the early morning — before 8am it's nearly empty, and the light on the water is extraordinary.

The Perfect 3-Day Florence Itinerary

Day 1

The Duomo, Uffizi & Ponte Vecchio

Start at dawn at the Duomo square — the marble facade in morning light with no crowds is one of Florence's great experiences. Climb the dome with your pre-booked pass. After lunch, head to the Uffizi (timed entry, pre-booked). Allow at least 3 hours. Evening walk across Ponte Vecchio and into Oltrarno for dinner in the backstreets of Santo Spirito.

Day 2

The David, Piazzale Michelangelo & San Miniato

Morning at the Accademia — arrive at your booked slot. After, explore the San Lorenzo market and the Medici Chapels. Afternoon: walk up to Piazzale Michelangelo for Florence's classic panoramic view, then continue to San Miniato al Monte church (free, extraordinary Romanesque interior, and the best sunset in Florence).

The Bardini Garden Secret

The Bardini Garden (€10) is Florence's most underrated viewpoint — a terraced hillside garden with unobstructed views of the Duomo. Almost no one goes here. Visit at 4pm when the afternoon light hits the terrace and the city glows below.

Day 3

Sant'Ambrogio Market, Oltrarno & The Negroni

Morning at Sant'Ambrogio market — more authentic than San Lorenzo, frequented by actual Florentines. Browse the artisan leather workshops on Via de' Neri. Afternoon at the Boboli Gardens. Late afternoon aperitivo at a wine bar (enoteca) — and order a Negroni. It was invented in Florence in 1919 at Caffè Casoni. You're in the right city for it.

Tuscany Day Trips

Florence is the ideal base for Tuscany. Every one of these is reachable in under 2 hours.

Siena

Italy's finest medieval city. The Piazza del Campo and the striped Duomo are both extraordinary. 90 mins by bus.

Cinque Terre

Five colourful coastal villages connected by hiking trails. 2.5 hrs by train to La Spezia, then local trains between villages.

Chianti Wine Country

Rent a car and drive the Via Chiantigiana through vine-covered hills. Stop for tastings in Greve in Chianti.

Food & Drink in Florence

Florentine cuisine is confident and unfussy. The Bistecca Fiorentina defines the city — a thick-cut T-bone from Chianina cattle, charcoal-grilled, served rare. Do not ask for it well done. You will be judged.

Bistecca Fiorentina

T-bone, charcoal, olive oil. Florence's defining dish.

Schiacciata

The Florentine flatbread — olive oil, rosemary, sea salt.

Chianti Classico

Order this, not just "Chianti". The Classico designation matters.

Artisan Gelato

Gelateria dei Neri — among the best in the city.

Oltrarno — The Real Florence

Cross the Ponte Vecchio and you enter a different Florence. Fewer tourist restaurants, more artisan workshops, better aperitivo bars. The neighbourhood of Santo Spirito in particular feels closer to the Renaissance city than anything on the north bank.

Walk Via Maggio for antique shops, Via dei Serragli for local bars, and Piazza di Santo Spirito at any hour for authentic neighbourhood life.

Where to Stay

Centro Storico — Walk to everything, premium price. Oltrarno — More atmospheric, better value, local character. Santa Croce — Well-connected, great restaurants, slightly less expensive than the historic centre. Avoid booking a hotel primarily by proximity to Santa Maria Novella station — the area is convenient but characterless.

Practical Tips

  • The Duomo Combined Pass (€30) covers the dome climb, Baptistery, Campanile, crypt and museum — book the dome slot when you buy it at museumflorence.com.
  • Many state museums offer free entry on the first Sunday of each month. Queues are long but worth it if you're flexible.
  • Florence gets oppressively hot in July and August. Start sightseeing before 9am and take a long lunch break in shade.
  • The Mercato Centrale (first floor) is excellent for a quick, high-quality, reasonably-priced lunch — not a tourist trap despite its fame.
