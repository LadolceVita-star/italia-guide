The Amalfi Coast is one of the world's most dramatic stretches of coastline — 50km of cliffs dropping straight into the Tyrrhenian Sea, lined with lemon groves, bougainvillea, and medieval villages that cling to the rock as if by sheer determination. It is also one of Italy's most visited destinations, which means timing and planning matter enormously. Come in May or September and it feels like paradise. Come in August and it can feel like a car park on a cliff.

The Towns — Where to Stay & What to See

1

Positano

The most photographed village on the coast — pastel houses cascading down a steep hillside to a pebble beach. Famously expensive but justifiably beautiful. Stay here if budget allows; visit as a day trip if not. The beach is crowded in summer; the backstreets above it are always worth exploring.

2

Amalfi

The largest town on the coast and its historic capital — once a powerful maritime republic that rivalled Venice. The Arab-Norman cathedral is extraordinary. More affordable than Positano, with better transport connections, making it the most practical base for exploring the coast.

3

Ravello

600 metres above the sea, away from the coastal crowds — the most refined and peaceful town on the Amalfi Coast. Villa Rufolo's terrace garden is one of the great views in Italy. Wagner composed part of Parsifal here. Richard Wagner, D.H. Lawrence, Virginia Woolf, and Gore Vidal all lived or stayed here.

4

Praiano & Cetara

The coast's best-kept secrets. Praiano sits between Positano and Amalfi with a fraction of the crowds and some of the coast's best swimming spots. Cetara, at the eastern end, is a working fishing village famous for its anchovy colatura (sauce) and completely untouristy restaurants.

The SS163 — One of the World's Great Drives

The Strada Statale 163 hugs the coastline for 40km between Positano and Vietri sul Mare — two lanes of tarmac cut into sheer cliffs above the Mediterranean, with blind corners, oncoming buses, and views that make you forget to breathe. Spectacular and terrifying in equal measure.

Driving the Amalfi Coast — What You Need to Know

The coast road is narrow, winding, and shared with buses, vans, scooters, and pedestrians. It is entirely doable — but requires full attention and some preparation.

Driving Tips

Drive westward (Salerno to Positano) — this puts you on the cliff side of the road with the sea views, rather than the mountain wall. Most rental cars are returned to Naples or Rome, so driving east-to-west is natural.

Start before 9am — the road becomes genuinely difficult in summer afternoons when tour buses and rental cars peak. Early morning is dramatically easier and more beautiful.

Park and walk — in Positano and Amalfi, park at the entrance of town (there are paid car parks) and walk down. Driving into the centre is nearly impossible in peak season.

No Car? No Problem

The SITA bus runs the entire coast road and is how locals travel between towns — cheap (€2.50 per journey), regular, and an experience in itself. Ferries also connect the main towns by sea from April to October — the view of Positano from the water is the best of all. Buy a combined ferry-and-bus pass for maximum flexibility.

The Perfect 3-Day Amalfi Coast Itinerary

Day 1

Arrive in Positano — Explore & Swim

Arrive by ferry from Naples or Salerno for the most dramatic first impression. Walk down through the vertical streets to the Spiaggia Grande. Swim, then explore the boutiques and ceramic shops on Via dei Mulini. Sunset from the terrace of the Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta. Dinner in the backstreets above the beach — avoid anything directly on the seafront where prices are highest.

Day 2

Amalfi Town & Ravello

Morning in Amalfi town — visit the Cathedral of Sant'Andrea (the Arab-Norman facade is extraordinary), walk through the Paper Museum, and buy limoncello directly from a producer on Via delle Cartiere. Take the bus up to Ravello after lunch — Villa Rufolo's terrace garden, with its view over the entire coast, is one of Italy's most beautiful spots. Return to Amalfi for dinner.

Day 3

Boat Trip & Hidden Coves

Rent a small boat for the day (no licence required for vessels under 40HP) or join a guided boat tour. Visit the sea caves between Positano and Praiano, swim at the Grotta dello Smeraldo (Emerald Grotto), and find one of the hidden coves that are inaccessible by road. Stop at Cetara for lunch — grilled fish and anchovy pasta in a restaurant that has never heard of tourist menus.

Beaches on the Amalfi Coast

Most Amalfi Coast beaches are pebble rather than sand, and many require paying for a sun lounger and umbrella (€15–30/day). The main exception is the wilder and harder-to-reach coves accessible only by boat or path.

  • Spiaggia Grande, Positano — the iconic beach, beautiful but busy. Free section at the edges, paid in the centre.
  • Spiaggia di Praiano — smaller, less visited, better value than Positano. The Marina di Praia cove is charming.
  • Furore Fjord — a tiny beach at the bottom of a dramatic gorge, only a handful of people can fit. Worth the walk down.
  • Cetara beach — a working fishing port beach with no tourist infrastructure and excellent local restaurants nearby.

Getting There

The nearest major transport hubs are Naples (1 hour from Positano by ferry or car) and Salerno (30 minutes from Amalfi by ferry). From Rome, take a high-speed train to Naples (1h 10m), then ferry or SITA bus to the coast. Flying into Naples (NAP) airport gives direct bus and ferry connections.

Food & Drink

  • Limoncello: The coast's famous lemon liqueur — made from the giant sfusato amalfitano lemons that grow on the terraced hillsides. Buy it from a producer, not a tourist shop.
  • Scialatielli: The Amalfi Coast's own pasta shape — thick, short, slightly irregular — typically served with mixed seafood or clams.
  • Alici di Cetara: Cetara's salted anchovies and colatura (anchovy sauce) are among the finest in Italy. Try them on bruschetta or stirred into pasta.
  • Fresh fish: Any restaurant away from the main tourist strips will serve the day's catch at reasonable prices. Ask what's fresh.

Practical Tips

  • Book accommodation months ahead for July and August — the coast has limited beds and they fill fast. May, June and September are easier and dramatically more pleasant.
  • The SITA bus from Sorrento to Amalfi is the most scenic and affordable way to travel the coast without a car — €2.50 per ride, runs frequently.
  • Ferry services operate April–October between Positano, Amalfi, Salerno and the islands of Capri and Ischia.
  • Wear proper shoes — the stepped streets of Positano are steep and slippery. Sandals are fine on flat ground; trainers are better everywhere else.
  • Combine with Naples (pizza, archaeology museum, Pompeii) and Capri (a ferry from Amalfi or Positano) for a complete southern Italy itinerary.
