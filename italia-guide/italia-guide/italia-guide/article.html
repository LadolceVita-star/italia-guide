The Perfect 7-Day Rome Itinerary — From Ruins to Restaurants

May 2026 · 12 min read · Updated for 2026
Advertisement

Seven days in Rome sounds like plenty of time. It isn't — not if you try to see everything. Rome rewards those who go deep rather than wide: linger in a piazza, get lost in Trastevere, discover a church that isn't on any list. This itinerary gives you the unmissable highlights while leaving room to actually feel the city.

At a Glance

Duration
7 days / 6 nights

First-time visitors, history lovers, food enthusiasts

Day 1 — Ancient Rome

Day 1

The Colosseum, Forum & Palatine Hill

Book the earliest available Colosseum slot. The same ticket covers the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill — walk them in that order. Finish with an aperitivo in Testaccio, Rome's most local neighbourhood, and dinner at a neighbourhood trattoria.

Booking Tip

Buy tickets at coopculture.it — not from resellers outside the gate. Book 2–3 weeks ahead in shoulder season, a month ahead in summer. The combined ticket (€18) covers all three sites.

Day 2 — Vatican City

Day 2

Vatican Museums, Sistine Chapel & St Peter's

Devote the full day. Start with a pre-booked Vatican Museums slot (museivaticani.va, ~€25). Allow 3–4 hours for the galleries and Sistine Chapel. St Peter's Basilica is free but has a dress code — shoulders and knees covered. Climb the dome for the best rooftop view in Rome. Dinner in Prati, the quiet neighbourhood outside the Vatican walls.

Day 3 — Baroque Rome & Piazzas

Day 3

Pantheon, Piazza Navona & Trastevere

Morning at Campo de' Fiori market, then walk to the Pantheon (€5 — genuinely one of the most extraordinary buildings in the world). Lunch near Piazza Navona. Afternoon at the Trevi Fountain — go early morning if you want it relatively quiet. Evening in Trastevere for dinner and a walk through its cobblestone streets.

Day 4 — Borghese & the Spanish Steps

Day 4

Borghese Gallery & Villa Borghese Gardens

The Borghese Gallery is one of Rome's finest museums and one of its most overlooked. Entry is strictly 2 hours and capped at 360 visitors per slot — book at least a month ahead. After, walk through the Villa Borghese gardens to the Spanish Steps. Evening: aperitivo in Pigneto for a more local Rome experience.

The Knights of Malta Keyhole

On Aventine Hill, there's a keyhole in the gate of the Knights of Malta villa. Look through it and you'll see a perfectly framed view of St Peter's dome. No crowds, no queues, and one of Rome's best-kept secrets.

Day 5 — Day Trip: Ostia Antica or Tivoli

Day 5

Outside Rome — Ancient Ruins or Renaissance Gardens

Two excellent options: Ostia Antica (Rome's ancient harbour city, 40 mins by train, far less visited than Pompeii) or Tivoli (Villa d'Este's extraordinary Renaissance gardens and Hadrian's Villa). Both are reachable by public transport and make for a full-day excursion.

Days 6–7 — Slow Rome

Days 6–7

Markets, Neighbourhoods & What You Missed

Two final days to revisit favourites and discover overlooked corners: the Coppedè neighbourhood (Rome's strangest and most wonderful Art Nouveau district), Porta Portese flea market on Sunday mornings, the Capuchin Crypt near Piazza Barberini, and as many coffee bars as you can fit in.
