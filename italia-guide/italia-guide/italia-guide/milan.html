Milan is the city Italians go to when they want to get things done — and visitors who come expecting the romantic, unhurried Italy of Rome or Tuscany are often surprised by its energy, ambition, and modernity. But beneath the fashion boutiques and financial districts is a city of extraordinary art, one of the world's great cathedrals, and an aperitivo culture so embedded in daily life that it has its own hour. Milan rewards those who know where to look.

Top Attractions in Milan

1

The Duomo

One of the world's largest and most elaborate Gothic cathedrals — 600 years in the making, with 3,400 statues, 135 spires, and a rooftop terrace where you can walk among the pinnacles above the city. Climb to the roof (lifts or stairs, €15–22) for the finest view of Milan. Book online.

2

The Last Supper

Leonardo da Vinci's masterpiece, painted directly onto the wall of Santa Maria delle Grazie's refectory in 1495–98. Entry is strictly timed, 15 minutes per group, maximum 30 visitors. Book at cenacolovinciano.vivaticket.it — often sold out 2–3 months ahead. Non-negotiable advance booking.

3

Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

The world's oldest shopping mall (1877), with a spectacular iron-and-glass vaulted ceiling. Stand on the Toro — the bull mosaic in the floor — and spin on your heel for good luck (the locals do it). Prada, Gucci, Louis Vuitton are here; the prices are for watching, not buying.

4

Pinacoteca di Brera

Milan's finest art museum — Raphael's Marriage of the Virgin, Mantegna's extraordinary Dead Christ, Caravaggio, Titian. Less overwhelming than the Uffizi in scale, but with a collection of comparable quality. In the bohemian Brera neighbourhood, with the best café scene in Milan.

Above the Spires — The Duomo Rooftop

Walking among the white marble pinnacles and statues of the Duomo rooftop, with the Alps visible on clear days to the north and the city spreading in every direction below, is one of the most unusual and memorable experiences in Italy. Book online and take the lift — no need to climb 250 steps unless you want to.

Milan's Best Neighbourhoods

Brera

Milan's artsy bohemian village — cobblestone streets, independent galleries, excellent restaurants, and the Pinacoteca di Brera. The most atmospheric neighbourhood in the city.

Navigli

Milan's canal district — lined with aperitivo bars, restaurants, antique market on Sundays. The best place for an evening in Milan, especially in summer when tables spill onto the canal banks.

Porta Nuova

Milan's modern face — the Vertical Forest (Bosco Verticale), the Unicredit Tower, and the Piazza Gae Aulenti. A striking contrast to the historic centre and worth seeing for the architecture alone.

The Perfect 2-Day Milan Itinerary

Day 1

The Duomo, Galleria & Brera

Start at the Duomo before the crowds arrive — walk around the exterior first, then go inside (free) before buying your rooftop ticket. Cross the Piazza del Duomo to the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II — coffee at Café Biffi or Savini if budget allows, or any standing bar otherwise. Walk north to the Brera neighbourhood for lunch. Afternoon at the Pinacoteca di Brera (allow 2 hours). Evening aperitivo in Brera — Camparino in the Galleria is the classic choice for a Campari Negroni.

Day 2

The Last Supper, Castello & Navigli

The Last Supper (pre-booked, specific time slot — arrive 15 minutes early, entry is refused if late). The church of Santa Maria delle Grazie is also worth seeing. Walk to the Castello Sforzesco — the Michelangelo Pietà Rondanini is inside, his final unfinished work, deeply moving. Lunch in Sempione park. Afternoon in the fashion district (Via Montenapoleone, Via della Spiga) — more for window-shopping than buying. Evening in the Navigli canals for aperitivo and dinner.

The Last Supper — Non-Negotiable Booking Rules

Book at cenacolovinciano.vivaticket.it, minimum 2 months ahead (often more in peak season). Each session lasts exactly 15 minutes. If you arrive late, you will not be admitted and will forfeit your ticket. There are no exceptions. Set an alarm.

Shopping & Fashion

The Quadrilatero della Moda

Milan's famous fashion rectangle — Via Montenapoleone, Via della Spiga, Via Sant'Andrea, and Corso Venezia — contains the flagship stores of every major Italian and international fashion house. Prada, Gucci, Versace, Armani, Dolce & Gabbana are all here.

For more accessible shopping, Corso Buenos Aires is Milan's high-street equivalent — 1km of shops at ordinary prices. The Navigli antique market (last Sunday of the month) is excellent for vintage clothing and design objects.

Food & Aperitivo in Milan

Milanese cuisine is richer and more northern than the rest of Italy — risotto, ossobuco, and cotoletta (the original Wiener Schnitzel) define the city's food identity. But Milan's true culinary institution is aperitivo.

  • Aperitivo: Milan invented the concept of free food with drinks — order a Campari Spritz or Negroni between 6–9pm and most bars bring out a spread of nibbles, sometimes elaborate enough to replace dinner. The Navigli district has the best aperitivo scene.
  • Risotto alla Milanese: Saffron risotto, golden and creamy — the city's signature dish. Order it at a traditional trattoria, not a tourist restaurant. Risotto al salto (leftover risotto fried into a crispy cake) is even better.
  • Cotoletta alla Milanese: Breaded veal cutlet, pan-fried in butter — flatter and wider than the Austrian version. The bone should be left on. Try it at Trattoria del Nuovo Macello.
  • Panettone: Milan's Christmas cake — available year-round from good bakeries. Pasticceria Marchesi (Via Santa Maria alla Porta) makes one of the finest in the city.

Practical Tips

  • Milan has two airports — Malpensa (MXP), 50km from the centre (Malpensa Express train, 40 mins, €13), and Linate (LIN), 7km from the centre (bus, 30 mins, €5). Most international flights use Malpensa.
  • The metro (4 lines) is efficient and covers the main sights. A 24-hour pass (€7) is good value if you're moving between neighbourhoods.
  • Fashion Week runs in February and September — hotels double in price and the city is overwhelmed. Avoid these weeks unless fashion is the specific reason to visit.
  • Milan is the best base for day trips to the Italian Lakes — Lake Como (1 hour by train to Como), Lake Maggiore (1 hour), and Lake Garda (1.5 hours to Desenzano).
  • The city's nightlife is centred on the Navigli and Brera districts. Bars stay open until 2am; clubs until dawn.
