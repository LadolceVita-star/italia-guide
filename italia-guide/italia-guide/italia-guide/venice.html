Venice is unlike anywhere else on earth — 118 islands connected by 400 bridges, where the only roads are canals and the only traffic is boats. For all its crowds, the city retains a profound, melancholy beauty that hits you the moment you step off the train and see the Grand Canal. The key to loving Venice is knowing how to find it beneath the surface.

Top Attractions in Venice

1

St Mark's Basilica

The golden Byzantine masterpiece at Venice's heart. Book skip-the-line entry at basilicasanmarco.it (€3). Arrive when it opens — the golden mosaics in morning light with no crowds is an experience few travellers get to have.

2

Doge's Palace

The seat of Venetian power for 1,000 years. Tintoretto's Paradise is the largest oil painting in the world. Book timed entry online. The Bridge of Sighs is inside — far more atmospheric than the view from the canal bridge outside.

3

Grand Canal by Vaporetto

Take vaporetto line 1 (the slow boat) from Piazzale Roma to San Marco. Sit at the front or back for 45 minutes of unobstructed views of Renaissance and Gothic palaces sliding past. The greatest boat ride in Europe.

4

Getting Deliberately Lost

Put your phone away and wander. The real Venice — laundry-hung calli, quiet campi, neighbourhood bakeries — only reveals itself when you stop following a map. Set aside at least half a day for this. You won't regret it.

Venice Before 8am — A Different City

The day-trippers arrive at 10am. Stay overnight and you'll have the canals, the piazzas, and the light entirely to yourself in the early morning. St Mark's Square at dawn — with only pigeons for company — is one of the great travel experiences in Europe.

How to Visit Venice Without the Crowds

Venice receives over 20 million visitors a year in a city built for 60,000 people. The crowds are real — but they're completely predictable and avoidable with a little planning.

Stay Overnight — It Changes Everything

The single most important piece of advice for Venice: stay at least one night. Day-trippers see a crowded, over-commercialised version of the city. Overnight guests — who are there before 8am and after 7pm — experience something close to magic.

The evening light on the canals, the sound of water lapping against stone in the silence of a back-canal, dinner at a bacaro without a tourist in sight — none of this is available to day visitors.

The Best Crowd-Free Timing

Visit St Mark's Basilica when it opens at 9am (pre-booked). Explore the Dorsoduro and Cannaregio sestieri before 11am. Return to popular areas after 7pm. Avoid the Rialto Bridge between 10am and 5pm entirely — walk 5 minutes to the Ponte degli Scalzi or Ponte dell'Accademia instead.

The Perfect 2-Day Venice Itinerary

Day 1

St Mark's, Grand Canal & Dorsoduro

St Mark's Basilica at opening (9am, pre-booked skip-the-line). Then Doge's Palace. Lunch away from the square — walk 5 minutes in any direction for half the price and double the quality. Afternoon: vaporetto line 1 along the Grand Canal to Ca' Rezzonico and explore Dorsoduro — home to the Peggy Guggenheim Collection and the best aperitivo bars in Venice. Evening cicchetti and ombre (small glasses of wine) at a bacaro in Dorsoduro or Cannaregio.

Day 2

Getting Lost, Rialto Market & Burano Island

Morning: take a traghetto (the €2 standing gondola ferry) across the Grand Canal — the cheapest gondola experience in Venice, used by locals daily. Explore Cannaregio and the morning Rialto Market (best before 10am, buy fruit and local snacks). Afternoon: vaporetto to Burano island (45 mins) for its extraordinary painted houses and the best lace in Italy. Return to Venice for sunset from Ponte dell'Accademia. Final dinner in Castello — the least touristy of the central neighbourhoods.

The Lagoon Islands

Venice's lagoon holds dozens of islands, each with a distinct character. Three are essential.

Burano

Brightly painted houses and extraordinary lace-making tradition. One of Italy's most photogenic places. Vaporetto 12 from Fondamente Nove, ~45 mins.

Murano

Venetian glass-making since 1291. Watch masters work, visit the Glass Museum, buy directly from studios. 10 mins by vaporetto from Fondamente Nove.

Torcello

Near-deserted, atmospheric, with a stunning Byzantine cathedral. Almost no tourists. The most haunting and beautiful island in the lagoon.

Food & Drink in Venice

Venetian cuisine is defined by the sea and the lagoon — clams, cuttlefish, salt cod, and spider crab are staples. The cicchetti culture is unique to Venice and should be experienced at least once.

Cicchetti

Venetian tapas — the authentic way to eat in Venice.

Aperol Spritz

Invented in Venice. Drink it here, in a campo, at 6pm.

Rialto Market

Operating since the 11th century. Go before 10am.

Sarde in Saor

Sweet-sour sardines — Venice's signature dish.

  • Bacari: Traditional Venetian wine bars — Al Timon, All'Arco near Rialto, and Cantina Do Mori (Venice's oldest bacaro, open since 1462) are the classics.
  • Where to eat: Cannaregio and Dorsoduro for neighbourhood restaurants. Castello for the least touristy experience in the central sestieri.
  • What not to order: Any fish that clearly came from a freezer, any pizza near St Mark's, anything with a menu displayed in 8 languages at the door.

Dorsoduro — Venice's Best Neighbourhood

The south bank of Venice's main island is where the city's university is based — which means young locals, good bars, and genuine neighbourhood energy. The Zattere waterfront faces the Giudecca canal and is perfect for an afternoon walk or a gelato in the sun.

The Peggy Guggenheim Collection sits right on the Grand Canal here — an unmissable museum in an unmissable location.

Getting Around Venice

No cars, no buses, no bikes — Venice navigates entirely on foot and by boat. The vaporetto (water bus) covers the whole city and islands. A 24-hour pass costs €25; 48-hour €35. For most journeys within the historic centre, walking is faster. You can cross from the train station to St Mark's on foot in about 35 minutes.

Practical Tips

  • Venice introduced a day-tripper entry fee (€5) in 2026 on peak days. Check veneziaunica.it before your visit.
  • Accommodation is significantly more expensive in Venice than other Italian cities. If budget is a concern, stay in Mestre on the mainland (10 mins by train) — but staying inside Venice itself is worth the premium.
  • Acqua alta (high water flooding) happens mainly November–January. Check tide.venezia.it if visiting in winter. The flooding is usually brief and the city provides raised walkways.
  • Download Google Maps offline before arriving — signal in the narrow calli can be unreliable.
  • Bring comfortable shoes with ankle support. The bridge steps are numerous, often uneven, and unavoidable.
