Rome rewards those who wander without a rigid plan — but a few must-see sights deserve advance booking. Three thousand years of history, art, food, and street life make the Eternal City the most layered destination in Italy. Here's how to experience the best of it.

Top Attractions in Rome

Rome's greatest hits are spread across the city but connected by walkable streets. Get these booked before anything else.

1

The Colosseum

The world's most iconic amphitheatre. Book tickets 2–3 weeks ahead at coopculture.it and arrive at opening for the best light and fewest crowds. Combined ticket includes the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill.

2

Vatican Museums

Home to the Sistine Chapel and 7km of galleries. Pre-book timed entry and budget at least half a day. Go early — the afternoon crowds make the experience exhausting.

3

Trevi Fountain

Go at 7am for a serene experience — by 10am it's shoulder-to-shoulder. Toss a coin with your right hand over your left shoulder to guarantee a return to Rome.

4

Borghese Gallery

The finest Bernini sculptures in the world, in a stunning villa surrounded by Rome's largest park. Entry strictly 2 hours, 360 visitors maximum — book at least a month ahead.

Ancient Rome — 2,000 Years of History

The Roman Forum, Palatine Hill and Colosseum are covered by one combined ticket — plan at least 4 hours to do all three justice.

The Perfect 5-Day Rome Itinerary

Five days is the sweet spot for Rome — enough for the highlights with time to sit in a piazza, get lost in Trastevere, and actually feel the city rather than just photograph it.

Day 1

Ancient Rome — Colosseum, Forum & Palatine Hill

Book the earliest Colosseum slot. The same ticket covers the Roman Forum directly next door and Palatine Hill above it — walk them in that order for a natural flow. Finish the afternoon in Testaccio, Rome's most authentic neighbourhood, for aperitivo and dinner at a neighbourhood trattoria.

Day 2

Vatican City — Museums, Sistine Chapel & St Peter's

Devote the full day. Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel (pre-booked, ~€25, 3–4 hours). St Peter's Basilica is free but enforce the dress code — shoulders and knees covered. Climb the dome for the best rooftop view in Rome. Dinner in Prati, the quiet residential neighbourhood outside the Vatican walls.

Day 3

Baroque Rome — Pantheon, Piazza Navona & Trastevere

Morning at Campo de' Fiori market, then the Pantheon (€5 — one of the most extraordinary buildings ever constructed). Lunch near Piazza Navona. Afternoon: Trevi Fountain before 9am or after 8pm for manageable crowds. Evening in Trastevere — cobblestone alleys, ivy-covered facades, and the best neighbourhood restaurants in Rome.

The Knights of Malta Secret

On Aventine Hill, there's a keyhole in an ornate green gate. Look through it and you'll see a perfectly framed view of St Peter's dome at the end of a garden corridor. Free, no queues, and one of Rome's most magical experiences. Via di Santa Sabina — ask a local if you can't find the gate.

Day 4 & 5

Borghese Gallery, Spanish Steps & Neighbourhood Days

Morning at Borghese Gallery (strictly pre-booked, 2 hours). Walk through Villa Borghese gardens to the Spanish Steps. Day 5: slow Rome — Sant'Ambrogio market, the Coppedè Art Nouveau neighbourhood, the Capuchin Crypt, and as many standing coffee bars as you can fit in. The nasoni drinking fountains across the city provide free, ice-cold water all day.

Food & Drink in Rome

Roman cuisine is unapologetically simple. Four classic pasta shapes define the city's food identity — and none of them involve cream.

Carbonara

Guanciale, egg yolk, black pepper. Never cream.

Trattoria Culture

Family-run restaurants in Trastevere & Testaccio.

Aperitivo Hour

Campari Soda or Negroni, 6–8pm, every evening.

Roman Gelato

Lids on containers = the real artisan gelateria.

  • Breakfast: Stand at the bar. Cornetto + cappuccino, €1.50–2. Sitting down triples the price.
  • The four Roman pastas: Carbonara (Trattoria Da Enzo), Cacio e Pepe (Tonnarello), Amatriciana (Da Remo), Gricia (any neighbourhood trattoria in Testaccio).
  • Suppli: Rome's street food — fried rice balls with mozzarella. Get them from Supplì Roma near Campo de' Fiori, €2.50 each.
  • Gelato: Fatamorgana (creative flavours), Fior di Luna (traditional), Della Palma (classic tourist favourite, still good). Avoid anything near major tourist sights.

Trastevere — Rome After Dark

The neighbourhood across the Tiber is where Romans actually spend their evenings. Ivy-draped medieval lanes, tiny piazzas filled with outdoor tables, and trattorias that have been in the same family for generations. Walk here rather than take a taxi — crossing the river at sunset is part of the experience.

Best streets: Via della Lungaretta, Piazza di Santa Maria in Trastevere, and the quiet backstreets behind the basilica.

Getting There

Most international flights arrive at Fiumicino (FCO), 30km from the centre. The Leonardo Express train (€14, 32 minutes, runs every 30 mins) goes directly to Roma Termini. Avoid the overpriced private "transfer" services — the train is faster, cheaper, and simpler. Ciampino (CIA) serves budget airlines; take a shuttle bus to Termini (~45 mins, €5–7).

Where to Stay

Centro Storico — Walk to everything, premium price. Trastevere — Atmospheric, great for evenings, slightly removed from main sights. Prati — Near Vatican, quieter, excellent value. Monti — Rome's hippest neighbourhood, artisan shops and excellent restaurants, 10 minutes from the Colosseum. Avoid the Termini area unless budget is the only consideration.

Practical Tips

  • Carry €100–200 cash — many small bars, markets, and trattorias prefer it.
  • Dress code for all churches: shoulders and knees covered. A lightweight scarf solves this permanently.
  • The nasoni (small drinking fountains found throughout the city) provide free, excellent drinking water — bring a refillable bottle.
  • Validate your transit ticket before boarding every time. Inspectors do check, and the fine is €100.
  • Buy a local SIM card (TIM or WindTre) at Roma Termini on arrival for affordable data.
