Umbria is Tuscany without the tourists — and for many travellers, it's the better version. Landlocked in the centre of Italy, it holds some of the country's most dramatic hilltop towns, the most extraordinary collection of medieval frescoes outside Florence, the birthplace of St Francis, black truffles from Norcia, and Sagrantino wine from Montefalco. It remains Italy's most underrated region by some distance.

Why Umbria Is Italy's Most Underrated Region

Umbria is the only Italian region with no coastline and no major city — which is precisely why it has remained so unspoiled. The landscape of green hills, ancient oak woods, and limestone ridges is immediately calming. Every hilltop seems to have a medieval town; every valley seems to hide a Romanesque church. Visitors who come expecting a second-rate Tuscany leave realising they've found something better: a region that hasn't yet learned to perform for tourists.

The Green Heart of Italy

Umbria's nickname — Il Cuore Verde d'Italia — is entirely deserved. Drive any road between Perugia and Spoleto and the landscape shifts constantly: oak forests giving way to olive groves, then vineyards, then the sudden drama of a hilltop town appearing on the horizon.

Perugia — Umbria's Capital

Perugia sits on a ridge 450 metres above the surrounding plain, its medieval centre strung along a single main street — the Corso Vannucci — with extraordinary views in every direction. It's a lively university city (home to two universities and thousands of international students) with a serious food culture, excellent museums, and Etruscan city walls older than Rome.

1

Corso Vannucci & Piazza IV Novembre

Perugia's elegant main street runs between two piazzas and is closed to traffic all day. The Fontana Maggiore, one of Italy's finest medieval fountains, anchors Piazza IV Novembre. The Palazzo dei Priori houses the excellent National Gallery of Umbria.

2

Etruscan Walls & Arch

Perugia's Arco Etrusco dates from the 3rd century BC — older than most of Rome. The lower city retains stretches of Etruscan walls, and the Ipogeo dei Volumni (an Etruscan tomb complex, 6km from the centre) is one of the most important Etruscan sites in central Italy.

3

Perugina Chocolate

Perugia is the home of the Baci chocolate — the iconic hazelnut and dark chocolate kisses wrapped with a love note. Visit the Perugina factory and museum for the full story and free tastings. The Eurochocolate Festival in October draws 900,000 visitors annually.

4

National Gallery of Umbria

The finest collection of Umbrian painting — Perugino (Raphael's teacher), Pinturicchio, Fra Angelico — housed in the spectacular Palazzo dei Priori. Often overlooked by visitors rushing to Assisi, it's one of central Italy's great art museums.

Assisi — Birthplace of St Francis

Assisi is one of the most moving places in Italy — and not only for religious visitors. The town's perfect preservation, its pink-stone houses cascading down Monte Subasio, and the extraordinary Basilica di San Francesco make it one of the most beautiful hilltop towns in Europe. UNESCO has protected the entire town since 2000.

Basilica di San Francesco

The double basilica built over St Francis's tomb is one of the great artistic achievements of the medieval world. The Upper Basilica contains Giotto's celebrated fresco cycle depicting the life of St Francis — 28 scenes painted around 1300 that effectively invented the Western painting tradition.

The Lower Basilica is darker and more intimate, with frescoes by Cimabue and Pietro Lorenzetti. Both are free to enter. Dress modestly — shoulders and knees covered, silence observed. This is an active place of worship, not only a museum.

Assisi Practical Note

Assisi is extremely busy in summer and on religious feast days. Visit on a weekday rather than a weekend, and arrive early — before the tour groups at 10am. The town itself is small; you can cover the main sights on foot in 3–4 hours, making it an ideal day trip from Perugia (25 mins by train) or Spoleto.

More Essential Umbrian Towns

Orvieto

Perched on a volcanic plug of tufa rock with a cathedral whose golden facade rivals any in Italy. The underground Etruscan tunnels beneath the town (guided tours available) add another layer of history. Excellent white wine — Orvieto Classico.

Spoleto

A Roman amphitheatre, a spectacular medieval aqueduct bridge, and one of Italy's best performing arts festivals (Festival dei Due Mondi, June–July). Less visited than Assisi or Orvieto — and all the more rewarding for it.

Norcia

The truffle and cured meat capital of Umbria. Every shop sells black truffles (winter), norcineria (the Norcino butchers who gave their name to a profession), and lentils from Castelluccio. A pilgrimage for serious food lovers.

Food & Wine in Umbria

Black Truffles

Norcia's winter truffles are among Italy's finest. Try truffle pasta, truffle eggs, truffle everything.

Sagrantino di Montefalco

One of Italy's most powerful red wines. Visit a winery in Montefalco for a tasting.

Lenticchie di Castelluccio

Tiny, sweet lentils from the Piano Grande plateau — among the finest in Italy.

DOP Olive Oil

Umbria produces some of Italy's finest extra-virgin olive oil — peppery, grassy, intense.

  • Truffle season: Black summer truffles (June–August), black winter truffles (November–March) — the finest. White truffles are rarer and found mainly in the north.
  • Sagrantino di Montefalco: One of Italy's most tannic and age-worthy red wines. Visit Arnaldo Caprai winery near Montefalco for the definitive experience.
  • Norcineria: Every town in Umbria has a norceria — a traditional pork butcher selling salumi, prosciutto, and sausages made in the Norcino tradition. Buy from local shops rather than tourist stands.

Getting Around Umbria

A rental car is strongly recommended for exploring Umbria fully. The hilltop towns are connected by winding country roads that buses serve infrequently. Perugia and Assisi are reachable by train from Rome and Florence, and Orvieto sits directly on the Rome–Florence main line — but travelling between Umbrian towns without a car is slow and limiting. Rent a car in Perugia or Florence and allow at least 3 full days to cover the region properly.

4-Day Umbria Itinerary

Day 1

Arrive in Perugia — Explore the Capital

Arrive in Perugia. Walk the Corso Vannucci from end to end, stopping at the Fontana Maggiore and the National Gallery of Umbria. Evening aperitivo at one of the bars on Piazza IV Novembre — Perugia's student population ensures excellent bar culture. Dinner at a local trattoria: order strangozzi al tartufo (local pasta with truffle).

Day 2

Assisi — St Francis & the Basilica

Drive or take the train to Assisi (25 mins). Start at the Basilica di San Francesco before the groups arrive — the Giotto frescoes in the Upper Basilica deserve at least an hour of unhurried attention. Walk up through the town to the Rocca Maggiore fortress for panoramic views over the Umbrian plain. Lunch in town, return to Perugia for the evening.

Day 3

Orvieto & the Montefalco Wine Road

Drive to Orvieto (1 hour). The cathedral facade is one of the most arresting in Italy — allow time for the interior (the Cappella di San Brizio frescoes by Luca Signorelli are outstanding). Visit the underground Etruscan tunnels. Return via Montefalco — stop at a Sagrantino winery for a tasting. Dinner back in Perugia or overnight in Montefalco.

Day 4

Norcia, Castelluccio & the Piano Grande

Drive east to Norcia (1.5 hours) — browse the norcinerie, buy truffle products and local salumi. Continue up to the Piano Grande di Castelluccio — a vast mountain plateau at 1,400m, extraordinary in any season but spectacular in late May–June when the wildflowers bloom (one of the most beautiful landscapes in Italy). Return to Perugia for departure.

Piano Grande Wildflowers

In late May and June, the Piano Grande di Castelluccio is carpeted with wildflowers — poppies, cornflowers, violets, and lentil flowers in a display that is genuinely one of the most beautiful natural spectacles in Italy. It's called the "fiorita" and draws photographers from across Europe. Book accommodation in Norcia months in advance for this period.
